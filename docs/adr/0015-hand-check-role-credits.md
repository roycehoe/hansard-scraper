# 0015. The hand check judges a chair credit by the sitting's chair marks

The hand check draws 1,000 credited rows and judges each credit against the Official Report (the site's ADR 0009). Its brief judges role credits ("The Chairman", "Deputy Speaker", "Mr Speaker", ministerial titles) against an office-holder table by date, built from outside sources, and calls a row the table cannot settle "record doesn't say", counted as wrong. "The Chairman" is not an office. The Speaker, a Deputy Speaker or any member who is not a Minister may take the chair without notice, and the role-names research of 13 October 2026 found no outside source that names one Chairman per row. A table by date cannot settle a bare "Mr Deputy Speaker" on a day with two Deputies in office either. Read that way, every such credit counts as wrong whatever the record prints. On 26 October 2026, 10,089 rows with a chairman label and 4,579 bare "Mr Deputy Speaker" rows were credited (`docs/data-loss-register.md`), about 15 in a 1,000-row sample, which would move the wrong-speaker ceiling from 0.30% to at least 1.69%. The check splits role labels in two. A label that names an office with one holder that day ("Mr Speaker", "The Prime Minister", a head of state, a Deputy Speaker on a day with one holder or one the printed Mr or Mdm picks out, a ministerial title) is judged by the table. The table is built from cited outside sources and not copied from `SPEAKER_TERMS` or `DEPUTY_SPEAKER_TERMS`, because stage 8 credited rows with those, so a copy would agree with any error they hold. A label that names the chair ("The Chairman", "Mr Chairman", a bare "Mr Deputy Speaker" on a two-Deputy day) is judged by the sitting's own chair marks: the last mark before the row and the first after it, from the same sitting, and any suspension or resumption line between. A mark is a "[X in the Chair]" note, an "X left the Chair" or "Thereupon ... the Chair" line, a chair label such as "Mr Speaker:", or the report's `MPs Speaking` tag of a chair role, which never overrides a nearer mark. A mark that names a person settles the credit, and a mark that names an office goes through the table. Anything else is "record doesn't say" and counts as wrong: no mark, a mark voided by a line between it and the row, marks before and after that name different people, or a mark that contradicts the credit. A conflict is never called right on the table alone. The report gives three counts: credits settled by a chair mark, by the table, and record doesn't say. The check cannot see a change of chair the record does not print, the limit ADR 0002's amendment of 17 October 2026 accepts for linking. The independent review that stood in for Royce during the autonomous planning run of 1 November 2026 made this ruling. Royce can overturn it before the report is published, and a change after publication means judging the role credits again.
