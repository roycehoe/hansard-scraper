# 0001. Attribution denominator

Speech attribution needs one rate that can reach 100% without hiding failures. Counting all speech rows can never reach 100%, because collective references like "Some hon. Members" name no one person and stage directions like "(Applause)" name no one at all. Counting only rows the resolver already understands would hide failures. The denominator is speech rows in Parliaments 0 to 11 that have a speaker string, minus collective references, which match any case-insensitive "hon. member(s)" form, and minus strings starting with "(" or "_". Parse noise, meaning section headers the parser mistook for speakers, stays in. That way a parser bug shows up as unlinked rows until someone fixes the parser. Parliament 12 is reported separately because its HTML fetch is incomplete and out of scope. Every published rate shows the all-rows figure next to it.

Note, 14 October 2026. The record now holds text and speech rows through 8 April 2025, and Parliament 12 is fetched whole. The headline stays Parliaments 0 to 11 so its history stays comparable. Parliaments 12, 13 and 14 are reported beside it, each with the same filter and its all-rows figure, and Parliament 12 is reported whole rather than as the partial fetch the paragraph above describes.
