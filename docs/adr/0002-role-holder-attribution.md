# 0002. Role-holder attribution

Many speeches name only a role, such as "Mr Speaker", "The Chief Minister" or "The Chairman", and the transcript never names the person. Stage 8 already links "Mr Speaker" and "The Chief Minister" to whoever held the role on the sitting date. The alternative was to leave every role-only string unlinked, which would leave tens of thousands of rows unattributed even though the holder is a matter of public record. A role-only string may be linked to a person when a cited source shows exactly one holder of that role on that sitting date. When the source is ambiguous, missing, or the role could have been filled by a stand-in that day, the row stays unlinked. A wrong link is worse than no link.

The printed honorific counts as evidence when it leaves exactly one holder. Hansard prints "Mdm Deputy Speaker" for a woman in the chair and "Mr Deputy Speaker" for a man, so on a day with one man and one woman in office the label names the person, unless the report text names her as a Deputy Speaker, in a chair annotation, a labelled "Mdm Deputy Speaker (Name)" row or the "MPs Speaking" header. A bare "Mr Deputy Speaker" on a day with two men links only when the report's own chair annotation or named speaker row names one of them, the report shows no other Deputy taking part, and nothing else printed that day names the other.

Changed 14 October 2026: the clause read "unless the report shows the woman taking part", which was stricter than the code. Stage 8 counts her as taking part only when the report text names her. The post-2012 load checked 143 Parliament 14 rows against this and found them right ([post-2012-load.md](../report/post-2012-load.md)).
