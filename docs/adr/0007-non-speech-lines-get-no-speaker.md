# 0007. Non-speech lines get no speaker

Status: accepted, 14 October 2026, extended 15 and 16 October 2026.

Context. An unbolded line after a member's turn inherited the member as speaker. Procedural lines ("Question put, and agreed to.", "Resolved,", "Adjourned accordingly at ..."), division lists, editorial notes and "rose" chamber events were stored as that member's speech. The records first called a chamber event a stage direction; [0009](0009-speech-kind-column.md) names it. The strata audit counted about 19,000 rows matching a procedural anchor and logged the error in 7 of every 10 budget and motion reports. In Parliaments 0 to 11, about 9,600 linked rows held text no member spoke.

Decision. Commit `be4d7dd` of 14 October 2026 gives no speaker to three kinds of line. After a procedural line the parser resets the current speaker, except after "Question proposed" and "*The same amendment also stood in the name of", where the mover often carries on. A whole-line editorial note gets no speaker: vernacular pointers, Standing Order 22(3) notes, chair notes, supply headers, "[Laughter]", chamber entries and copies handed in. A "rose" chamber event gets no speaker, in the bold form "**Mr X** rose -", the plain form "Mr X rose -" and the forms "rose and then resumed his seat" and "rose - [Interruption]". Commit `be4d7dd` did not keep the bold name, so the row read "rose -" with no speaker. Commit `9df9966` of 15 October 2026 keeps it: the row holds the bold name, the separator and the old text, still with no speaker. A targeted UPDATE applied the 14 October result to stored rows. Commit `b1de4f2` of 15 October 2026 adds recorded question decisions ("Question, 'That ...' put, and agreed to.") as procedural lines that keep the turn, and a "Clause" or "Schedule ordered to stand part" line that ends it.

Round 2 of the speech fidelity loop found the same defect in more shapes, and write #3 fixes them. Oath-roll entries and procedural one-liners ("Petition handed to the Clerk", "Bill read the First time") get no speaker (`bcb0af2`). Labelled assent, dissent and resumed-seat lines get none and end the turn (`1884d53`). Footnotes between rules and amendment text get none (`55e99a6`).

The user ruled on three more cases on 15 October 2026. A single-speaker collapsed row drops the leading report title, as a multi-speaker report does, and its procedural lines ("Adjourned accordingly at ...") become rows of their own with no speaker (`9209f01`, `517b2c1`). A direction in the middle of a member's paragraph is not attributed to the member. Gestures ("[Indicating ...]", "[Gesturing]") count as chamber events.

The first ruling on the mid-paragraph direction split the paragraph in source order into before, direction and after: three rows, where the before and after rows keep the speaker and the direction row has none. The independent decision review of the same day objected. The split cuts a sentence in two, which is the harm the F4 join ([0006](0006-paragraphs-joined-across-column-and-page-markers.md)) had just undone, and the cut rule that detects such cuts cannot see it, because a row with no speaker sits between the halves. The user approved a rework on 15 October 2026. The direction is cut out of the paragraph. The text before and after it joins with one space, so the sentence stays whole. The paragraph keeps its row, and each direction follows it as the next row, in source order, with no speaker and kind CHAMBER_EVENT. Nothing is duplicated. The position of the direction inside the paragraph is lost: the paragraph reads without it and the next row holds it. The rework is commit `9050403`. A full stop right after the brackets goes back to the sentence it ended, so two sentences do not glue into one. In a parse of the cached reports it changes 702 reports and leaves 1,153 fewer rows than the three-row split, with no text lost. The helpers named for stage directions are renamed for chamber events.

The newer system tags procedural paragraphs `(proc text)`. `_prepare_newer_system_html` deleted the markers and kept the text, and regexes in the speech parser then tried to rediscover what the source had already said. After the fixes above, 383 tagged paragraphs in 123 reports were still credited to a speaker outside collapsed rows, and 1,231 inside them. A pair that wraps a whole block now stays in the stored markdown as the literal text `[(proc text) ... (proc text)]` (commit `0e4a110`): 10,060 of the 10,107 pairs in the cached reports. The speech parser turns that line into a row with no speaker and kind PROCEDURAL, and decides turn handling on the untagged text as before. This overrides the earlier ruling of 15 October 2026 that kept a presenter's text inside a collapsed row: a tagged block gets no speaker inside a collapsed report too. A tagged block never joins its neighbour across a page marker. A pair that shares its block with other text, spans blocks or nests, and a lone marker, keep the old treatment: the marker is removed and the text stays with its line. Those often tag speech by mistake, as in reports 19304, 12651 and 18579. In a parse of the cached reports 2,762 markdowns change, 941 reports change rows and 1,132 credited rows become PROCEDURAL rows with no speaker. They include 20 bill reports where "The House immediately resolved itself into a Committee on the Bill. - [Name]" had lost its text and left a junk "]." row credited to the member; the text is restored.

Consequences. 12,245 stored rows lost their speaker and speaker_id, 11,135 of them linked. A re-parse of 41,697 reports moved 12,255 rows to no speaker and moved none to a different speaker. Four changed rows in the stale reports 26467 and 27699 could not be matched and were left alone. Hand reads of every line kind found real speech in 6 rows only: a Budget statement in report 22691, resumed after a suspension, was credited to Mr Speaker and is now unattributed. One heading fragment in report 13940 drops. The bold "rose" form covers 1,014 rows, 1,001 of them linked, each linked to the member with no speech in the row.

Until `9df9966` the chamber event kept no name. Who rose was gone from the row, though the raw HTML kept it, and round 2 counted 12 Tier A rows of "X rose -" with the name lost. Eight more "rose ---" rows lost their speaker on 15 October 2026 when the html2text escape fix exposed them, and the user accepted that. The question-decision rule covers 664 rows, from the handoff line that lists it with three other fixes.

Figures moved as follows (Parliaments 0 to 11): ADR 0001 rate 97.61% to 97.74%, all-rows rate 90.42% to 88.88%, text share 96.81% to 96.58%. The rate that rose is the one the ADR 0001 denominator rewards when a speaker becomes null, so quote all three. `docs/report/non-speech-repair/snapshot_before.tsv` holds every target's speaker and speaker_id before the write, which is enough to undo it.

The changes of 15 and 16 October 2026 move stored rows as follows. 2,973 single-speaker collapsed rows in 2,593 reports lose the leading title, and 1,036 procedural lines in collapsed rows become rows of their own with no speaker. 268 paragraphs lose an inline direction, which adds 3,573 chamber-event rows, and 71 gesture directions lose their speaker. In a parse of the cached reports, 1,132 credited rows in 941 reports become PROCEDURAL rows with no speaker, and 2,762 stored markdowns change. The ADR 0001 rate and the all-rows rate move with the new no-speaker rows. Quote all three figures after the write: 526,807 / 538,255 = 97.87%, 526,807 / 607,756 = 86.68% and 181,359,552 / 186,290,919 = 97.35%. A dropped title is not text lost from the record: `Report.title` and `Report.markdown_content` hold it.

Alternatives. Leave the speaker on these lines, as before: the all-rows rate and text share stay higher and the speaker index credits members with text they did not speak. Keep the chamber event's speaker: it would credit the member with a row that holds no speech of theirs, and nulling the bold form costs about 0.15 points of the all-rows rate with no speech lost. Keep the title in a collapsed row, as a row of its own or inside the speech: the user ruled it out, and the title is already in `Report.title`. Split a mid-sentence direction into three rows: ruled out by the review, because it cuts a sentence and hides the cut from the detector. Keep the direction inside the speaker's paragraph: the row would credit the member with text no member spoke, and the user ruled it Tier A. Keep a tagged paragraph inside a collapsed row, as the earlier ruling did: the source marks it as procedural. Keep every tagged pair, whatever else shares its block: those often tag speech by mistake. Add a row-type column to mark these rows: built, see [0009](0009-speech-kind-column.md).
