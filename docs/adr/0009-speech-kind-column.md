# 0009. Speech kind column

Status: accepted, 16 October 2026. Supersedes the lines in [0005](0005-text-before-the-first-speaker-dropped.md) and [0007](0007-non-speech-lines-get-no-speaker.md) that turned down a row-type column. Migration `7c1d4e9a2b60` (commit `e8b8804`) adds the column, and the parser has set it since `2eef141`. The replica and the remote were both migrated at 22:51 on 15 October 2026; the migration wrote no rows. Write #3 fills the column for stored rows, so stored rows have no kind yet.

Context. A NULL `speaker` stood for six different things: procedural lines, editorial notes and footnotes, chamber events such as "[Applause]", oath-roll entries, preamble text, and real speech the parser could not attribute. The last group includes the Budget statement in report 22691 ([0007](0007-non-speech-lines-get-no-speaker.md)) and the unlabelled opening speeches in reports 24248 and 31083 ([0008](0008-text-before-the-first-speaker-kept.md)). A researcher could not ask for speech with an unknown speaker apart from rows that are not speech. The metrics could not either. The all-rows rate and text share score a parser that gives up on a speech the same as one that correctly leaves a procedural line without a speaker. The independent decision review of 15 October 2026 made this its first finding, and the user approved the change.

[0005](0005-text-before-the-first-speaker-dropped.md) and [0007](0007-non-speech-lines-get-no-speaker.md) each turned down a row-type or confidence column with one reason: "out of scope in the peer-learnings brief, decision 1". That brief of 14 October 2026 set up two changes, more NULL speakers on non-speech lines and a read-only link-plausibility script, and listed the column outside that job. It bounded one repair. It did not weigh the column, and it could not weigh the later changes that put more text in NULL rows. The parser already knows the answer. Every branch of `_parse_speeches` that appends a row has just decided what kind of line it holds.

Decision. `speech.kind` is a Postgres enum, `speechkind`, with seven values. The labels are the member names in upper case, because SQLAlchemy persists the member name. `kind` says what a row's text is. It is one axis: content, never position. Where a row sits, such as in the preamble of a report, is not a kind.

| Kind | What the row holds |
|---|---|
| SPEECH | Words a member said or wrote. The speaker is known or NULL. |
| PROCEDURAL | A step in the House's business: a procedural anchor, a question decision, "Resolved,", "Adjourned accordingly", an oath roll, "Order read", a change of chair, "The House rose", a `(proc text)` paragraph. |
| CHAMBER_EVENT | Something that happened in the room and is not speech: applause, laughter, an interruption, a gesture, "X rose", "indicated assent", copies handed in or distributed. |
| EDITORIAL_NOTE | A note the reporter or editor added: a footnote between rules, a vernacular or Standing Order pointer, a table note. |
| DOCUMENT_TEXT | The text of a document quoted into the record: a motion, an amendment, a bill. |
| HEADING | A title or caption kept as a row: a supply head, a table caption, a "Head NNNN -" fragment. |
| UNCLASSIFIED | No speaker, and no rule recognised the row. |

A step that changes the state of the House's business is PROCEDURAL. A line that describes what someone did is CHAMBER_EVENT.

A row with a speaker is SPEECH. A row without one takes the kind of the rule that removed its speaker: a procedural anchor gives PROCEDURAL, an inline direction gives CHAMBER_EVENT, a footnote between rules gives EDITORIAL_NOTE. A no-speaker row that no rule recognises is UNCLASSIFIED, so no row is forced into a wrong kind. A few rules only name a kind and take no speaker away: amendment text ("That the sum ... allocated for Head"), order-paper questions ("To ask the Minister ...") and motion text ("That this House ...") are DOCUMENT_TEXT; "Order for Third Reading read", "resolved itself into a Committee" and the "Bill ... read", "Question ... put" and "reported" lines are PROCEDURAL; "Head NNNN -" and "Clause 5 -" captions are HEADING. The commit changed no speaker and no transcript on any of the 41,697 cached reports. A preamble row takes its kind by content like any other row; [0008](0008-text-before-the-first-speaker-kept.md) lists the cases.

SPEECH with a NULL speaker means the words were spoken and the speaker is unknown. It replaces the review's `unattributed_speech`. Whether the speaker is known is already the `speaker` column, so a second kind would record it twice. `data/unattributed_speech.json` names the rows that carry it. It lists, per report, the label the parser wrongly gives a range of rows and the first and last row of that range by transcript start. The populate stage gives the rows in a range that carry the listed label no speaker and kind SPEECH, and raises if the range is gone. The list has ten entries: the eight reports where a member resumes unlabelled after the chair grants leave (8732, 280, 16500, 17279, 6150, 18272, 5895 and 9972), the Budget speech in 31083, whose rows the parse already leaves without a speaker, and the mover's opening speech in 24248, 30 rows kept as rows with no speaker.

The [register](../data-loss-register.md) counts the stored rows under a wrong label: 465 rows in ten reports, the eight, 31083 and 42017. Write #3 sets their `speaker` and `speaker_id` to NULL. The right speaker is known for every row except those of 42017, and it is not stored. An override file with the right speakers is out of scope for write #3.

Report 42017 departs from the overnight brief, which gave its rows kind SPEECH. Its seven stored rows are a notice of motion that no member spoke, and the register's right speaker for them is none. The new parse already gives the notice no speaker, so the list leaves 42017 out and the parser's kind for it is UNCLASSIFIED. Write #3 sets those seven rows to speaker NULL, speaker_id NULL and kind UNCLASSIFIED. One 7-row UPDATE to kind SPEECH reverses the call.

`kind` is nullable now. Write #3 sets it from the final parse on every row of a report it rebuilds and of a report whose stored rows already equal the final parse, and on a row of a held report only where the stored row equals a parsed row. Any other row stays NULL and is listed: 4,176 rows in 170 reports in the rehearsal (168 held reports and the hand-edited 22647 and 22650). NOT NULL waits for a later migration, after those reports are rebuilt. The delta shipper sends `id` and `kind` for a row whose only change is its kind (405,345 rows), and `id`, `ordinal` and `kind` for a row whose only other change is its ordinal (599,073 rows).

Consequences. A query can count speech rows with no speaker apart from rows that are not speech: `kind = 'SPEECH'` and `speaker IS NULL` against the other NULL speakers. In the write #3 rehearsal that count is 488 rows in 10 reports. The count is a floor. In the rehearsal the whole table of 1,048,709 rows holds SPEECH 963,887 (488 of them with no speaker), PROCEDURAL 46,833, UNCLASSIFIED 20,579, CHAMBER_EVENT 5,794, DOCUMENT_TEXT 2,584, HEADING 2,570, EDITORIAL_NOTE 2,286 and NULL 4,176. The largest UNCLASSIFIED classes are division lists, oath-roll names and unanchored procedural lines. Speech that the parser leaves without a speaker and that no list names is also UNCLASSIFIED, because no rule recognised it. The three attribution numbers keep their definitions, and [0001](0001-attribution-denominator.md) still counts rows with a speaker string. A parser that gives up on speech now shows in the UNCLASSIFIED count and in the SPEECH-with-no-speaker count. A researcher who wants spoken words filters on `kind = 'SPEECH'`. Until NOT NULL, a NULL `kind` means no rule has classified the row, in a report write #3 left alone. It does not mean the row is not speech. `kind` is parser output like `speaker`, so a re-parse rewrites it.

Alternatives. Keep NULL and say nothing: the state before this record. The review's wider enum (speech, procedural, editorial, stage_direction, preamble, footnote, heading, caption, time): `preamble` puts position on a content axis, and a preamble row can be procedural or speech. Footnote and caption fit EDITORIAL_NOTE and HEADING, and time stamps get no row. A boolean `is_speech`: it cannot tell a procedural line from a footnote, and it has no place for a row no rule recognised. A separate table for non-speech rows: a researcher reading a report by ordinal would lose the rows between. NOT NULL now: a held report has rows the parser did not rebuild, and a guessed kind on them is worse than none. A confidence column: not built.
